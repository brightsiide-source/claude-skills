What to Know About Arizona Probate

Arizona probate is governed by ARS Title 14. The Superior Court in the county where the deceased lived handles the case — typically Maricopa County Superior Court (Phoenix metro) or Pima County Superior Court (Tucson). A few practical points that matter for selling:

  • Arizona has formal and informal probate. Most uncontested estates go through informal probate, which is faster (4-8 months typical) than formal probate (6-12+ months).
  • Small Estate Affidavit options. For real property under $100,000, AZ allows a Small Estate Affidavit (after a 6-month wait) that avoids probate entirely. For personal property under $75,000, the wait is 30 days.
  • The Personal Representative can sell during probate — usually with court approval (or independent authority granted in the will).
  • You don't have to clean it out. We buy properties with the deceased's belongings still in them. Take family photos, jewelry, important documents, anything sentimental. Leave the rest.
  • Out-of-state heirs are very common. Many Arizona estates pass to adult children living in California, the Midwest, or the Northeast. The entire transaction can be handled remotely — documents via overnight mail or remote online notarization.

Why Inherited Arizona Houses Don't Sell Well on the MLS

Some inherited houses are great MLS candidates. Most aren't. Common issues:

  • Decades of deferred maintenance. Roof past replacement age, HVAC systems past their warranty, dated 1970s/80s kitchens.
  • Stuff. 40+ years of life accumulated in the house. MLS expects empty and clean.
  • Multi-heir disagreements. Three siblings can't agree on price, paint color, or whether to list. A cash sale gives one number all parties can vote yes/no on.
  • Out-of-state executors who can't easily coordinate with agents, inspectors, and cleaners from another time zone.
  • Snowbird homes that were used 4 months a year for decades — surface wear-and-tear that hides bigger underlying issues.
