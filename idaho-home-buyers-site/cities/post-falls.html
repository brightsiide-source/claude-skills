Post Falls has grown into a city of roughly 45,000 residents wedged between Coeur d'Alene and Spokane, Washington, and much of that growth has come from commuters looking for a more affordable alternative to CDA's resort-driven prices. The Spokane River runs straight through town, powering the falls at Post Falls Dam and feeding Q'emiln Park, a favorite for kayakers and hikers. The city carries both a residential and an industrial identity — subdivisions have spread quickly on the east and south sides, while a solid manufacturing and distribution base, plus the outlet shopping near the interstate, anchors the local economy. That growth has been good for sellers of well-kept homes, but it has also made buyers more selective about condition, since move-in-ready inventory is easy to find nearby.

We buy houses in any condition — from tidy homes in newer subdivisions near the river to older properties closer to downtown that need real work. There are no agent commissions, no closing costs, and no repair bills to front. The offer we make is the amount you walk away with.

Post Falls carries its own local landmarks that shape how the town feels day to day. Post Falls Dam and the falls themselves sit right along the river downtown, drawing walkers and photographers, while Q'emiln Park just below the dam is a favorite launch point for kayakers running the Spokane River. Treaty Rock Historical Site preserves one of the area's oldest landmarks, a reminder of the town's roots before it became a bedroom community. The mix of that small-town history with a steady stream of new residents drawn by lower prices than Coeur d'Alene or Spokane creates a market where pace of sale often matters as much as price — sellers juggling a job change, a move, or a family situation need certainty more than they need to chase the highest possible number.

The Post Falls & Kootenai County Market, in Plain Terms

Post Falls sits inside the same Kootenai County market as Coeur d'Alene, but it prices differently because of its role as the more attainable, commuter-friendly option. Newer subdivisions toward the Prairie and Greensferry areas tend to trade close to replacement cost, while the older core near downtown and Spokane Street carries a mix of early-20th-century homes and mid-century construction. Toward Rathdrum and the outskirts of the city, lots grow larger and many properties shift to private wells and septic systems rather than municipal utilities. Because a large share of Post Falls buyers work across the state line in Spokane, local demand can shift with employment trends on the Washington side as much as with anything happening in Idaho.

As with the rest of the state, Idaho's status as a non-disclosure state means sale prices aren't part of the public record, which makes it harder for a national buyer to price a Post Falls home accurately. We work this market directly and price from what's actually selling here, not a remote algorithm.

A note on wells and septic around Post Falls

Many parcels toward Rathdrum and the outer edges of Post Falls rely on private wells and septic systems rather than city water and sewer. In a traditional sale, that often means well-flow tests, septic certifications, and repair demands that slow a closing down. We buy well-and-septic homes as-is and handle that due diligence ourselves, so it never becomes your delay or your bill.

Kootenai County Selling Notes

A few Idaho-specific facts that work in your favor when you sell:

  • No real estate transfer tax. Idaho does not charge a state or county transfer tax, so that line item simply doesn't exist on your closing statement.
  • Property taxes are paid in arrears. Kootenai County bills for the prior year, so the closing agent prorates what's owed — we take care of that split as part of a clean cash close.
  • Homeowner's exemption. If the Post Falls home has been your primary residence, Idaho's homeowner's exemption may have reduced its taxable value; that status gets settled at closing.
