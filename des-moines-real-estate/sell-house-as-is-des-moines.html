The Kinds of Properties We Buy

Most cash-buyer landing pages say "any condition." We actually mean it. A non-exhaustive list of properties we've closed on in the Des Moines metro:

  • Fire-damaged houses. Partial fire, full fire, smoke damage that the insurance settlement didn't cover. We buy them as they sit.
  • Code enforcement situations. Des Moines, Urbandale, and Polk County code enforcement notices that turned into "I just want this off my plate" decisions. We handle the violations after closing.
  • Hoarding properties. No judgment. Leave it all behind. We do the cleanout.
  • Foundation and structural issues. Cracked block, settling, water-table problems in the Walnut Creek floodplain, leaning porches, sagging rooflines.
  • Tornado, hail, and storm damage. Eastern Iowa weather is what it is. We buy properties before or after insurance claims, with or without insurance proceeds.
  • Long-vacant houses. Owner died, family moved out of state, house sat for 5+ years. Mold, broken pipes, animal damage. We buy them.
  • Mid-rehab houses. Started a flip, ran out of money or time, half-finished kitchen, walls open, permits expired. We take it from where you left off.
  • Tenant-occupied disasters. Bad tenants you can't evict, properties trashed by previous occupants, leases you'd rather not deal with. We can buy with tenants in place.

How As-Is Pricing Actually Works

Our offer is based on the property's after-repair value (ARV) — what it would sell for once renovated — minus our estimated rehab cost and a margin for our risk and work. So a distressed property in Beaverdale with $80k of needed repairs and a $300k ARV might net you something in the $170k-$200k range as a cash offer.

The condition doesn't change whether we'll buy it. The condition does change what we can pay — because we're the ones doing the rehab after closing.

We're transparent about this math. On the call, we'll show you our ARV estimate, our rehab estimate, and how we got to the offer. You can disagree with our numbers and shop the offer around — many sellers do, and we don't take it personally.

Honest Note: When MLS Beats As-Is Cash

If the issue is cosmetic — outdated kitchen, old carpet, dated bathrooms — you may be better off with a traditional listing, even with the time and commission. Investors compete hard for "needs cosmetic updates" houses in Beaverdale and West Des Moines and you'll see multiple offers.

If the issue is structural, environmental, or legal — that's our lane. MLS buyers' financing falls apart on those. We don't.
