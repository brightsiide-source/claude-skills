What Iowa Homeowners Should Know About Foreclosure

Iowa is a judicial foreclosure state, which means the lender has to file a lawsuit in district court to foreclose on your home. The process is governed primarily by Iowa Code Chapter 654. In Polk County, the average timeline from the lender's first filing to a sheriff's sale runs roughly 5 to 7 months — longer than in many states.

That timeline is actually good news, because it means there's almost always time to sell the house before the sheriff's sale — if you don't use a traditional MLS listing, which typically takes 60-90 days from list to close. Our process closes in 10-30 days.

Selling before the sheriff's sale is materially better than letting the foreclosure complete:

  • A foreclosure stays on your credit report for 7 years. A regular sale doesn't.
  • You keep any equity above what's owed. At a sheriff's sale, the property usually sells for just enough to cover the loan and legal fees — any equity you had is gone.
  • Your future borrowing options are preserved. Lenders treat a recent foreclosure very differently than a recent sale.
  • You control the timing — the closing date, what you take with you, when you move out.

Honest Note About Equity

If you're significantly underwater on the mortgage — meaning the payoff is more than the house is worth — a regular sale (or a short sale negotiated with your lender) is usually the better path, not a cash sale. We'll tell you that on the first call rather than waste your time.

If you have any equity, even just $5,000-$20,000, a cash sale before sheriff's sale usually nets you more than letting the foreclosure complete. At sheriff's auctions in Polk County, properties routinely sell for just the payoff amount — any remaining equity is lost.
