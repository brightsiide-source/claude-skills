Why Pueblo Homeowners Sell to Us

Pueblo is a Southern Colorado steel and railroad town — about 110,000 residents, working-family demographics, significantly more affordable than the Front Range to the north. Common seller patterns:

  • Older Pueblo housing stock — significant inventory built 1900s-1950s (Bessemer, Northside, downtown) with deferred maintenance, dated electrical, plumbing under slab, foundation issues from expansive clay.
  • Estate sales from longtime Pueblo families — multi-generational ownership being passed to adult children who live elsewhere.
  • Pre-foreclosure situations. Pueblo's median income runs below the Front Range; pre-foreclosure is more common here than in Denver or Boulder. Colorado's Public Trustee process runs 110-125 days; we close before auction.
  • Tired landlords with Pueblo rentals — many out-of-area investors bought cheap in the 2010s and are now done managing turnover.
  • Out-of-state inheritances. Pueblo's children moved north, south, and out of state for decades; many inherited properties are owned remotely.
  • Distressed properties — code citations from City of Pueblo, vacant building registration issues, hail damage, copper theft on long-vacant properties.
  • Steel-mill workforce relocations — EVRAZ Pueblo (formerly CF&I) layoffs and retirements drive turnover.
