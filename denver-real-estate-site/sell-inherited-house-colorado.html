What to Know About Colorado Probate

Colorado probate is governed by the Colorado Probate Code (Title 15). In the Denver metro and surrounding counties, estates are administered in district court for the county where the deceased lived (Denver, Larimer, Weld, Boulder, etc.).

  • Colorado has formal and informal probate. Informal probate is faster (typically 6-12 months) and requires less court involvement. Formal probate involves regular court hearings and is used for contested estates.
  • The personal representative can sell during probate with the proper court authority. For informal probate, this is typically straightforward; for formal probate, court approval may be required.
  • Small estate options. Colorado offers Collection of Personal Property by Affidavit for estates under $80,000 in personal property — though this generally doesn't cover real property.
  • You don't have to clean it out. We buy properties with the deceased's belongings still in them.
  • Out-of-state heirs are common in Colorado estates. The entire transaction can be handled remotely.

Why Inherited Colorado Houses Don't Sell Well on the MLS

  • Decades of deferred maintenance. Many Denver-area homes are 50-100+ years old.
  • Stuff. Years of life accumulated.
  • Multi-heir disagreements. A cash sale gives one number all parties can vote yes/no on.
  • Out-of-state executors who can't easily coordinate with agents from another time zone.
  • Properties with hail / weather damage common to the Front Range that MLS buyers' lenders won't touch.
