What Colorado Homeowners Should Know About Foreclosure

Colorado uses a unique Public Trustee foreclosure process that's different from both judicial-foreclosure states (like Iowa or Indiana) and traditional non-judicial states (like California or Arizona). It's governed by Colorado Revised Statutes Title 38, Article 38.

  • The Public Trustee is a county officer (one per county) who acts as a neutral third party between borrower and lender.
  • Notice of Election and Demand (NED) is filed with the Public Trustee — this starts the formal process.
  • The Rule 120 hearing is held in district court — a brief court review confirming the lender has the right to foreclose.
  • Public Trustee Sale typically happens 110-125 days after the NED filing.
  • Cure rights are extensive in Colorado — you can cure the default (pay arrears + costs) up to noon the day before the sale.

Selling before the Public Trustee Sale is materially better than letting the foreclosure complete:

  • A foreclosure stays on your credit report for 7 years.
  • You keep any equity above what's owed. At Public Trustee Sales, properties typically sell for just enough to cover the loan and fees.
  • Your future borrowing options are preserved.
  • You control the timing — closing date, move-out, what you take with you.

Honest Note About Equity

If you're significantly underwater on the mortgage — payoff greater than the house is worth — a short sale negotiated with your lender is usually better than a cash sale. We'll tell you that on the first call.

If you have any equity, even $10k-$30k, a cash sale before the Public Trustee Sale usually nets more than letting the foreclosure complete. At Public Trustee Sales, properties routinely sell for just the payoff amount.
