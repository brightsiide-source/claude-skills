What to Know About Indiana Probate

Indiana probate is governed by Indiana Code Title 29. In the South Bend area, estates are administered in St. Joseph Probate Court (or Elkhart Superior Court for Elkhart County estates). A few practical points:

  • Indiana offers both supervised and unsupervised probate. Unsupervised probate is faster (typically 4-8 months) and requires less court involvement. Supervised probate involves regular court approval for sales and other transactions.
  • The executor (or "personal representative") can sell during probate — with court approval for supervised cases, with their own authority for unsupervised cases.
  • You don't have to clean it out. We buy properties with the deceased's belongings still in them. Take family photos, jewelry, important documents, anything sentimental. Leave the rest.
  • Out-of-state heirs are common in St. Joseph County estates — many South Bend retirees' adult children live in Chicago, Indianapolis, or further. The entire transaction can be handled remotely.
  • Small Estate Affidavit available for estates under $100,000 (gross value of personal property) after a 45-day wait, which can bypass full probate.

Why Inherited South Bend Houses Don't Sell Well on the MLS

  • Decades of deferred maintenance. Many South Bend homes are 50-100+ years old — original kitchens, knob-and-tube wiring, aging boilers.
  • Stuff. 40+ years of life accumulated. MLS expects empty and clean.
  • Multi-heir disagreements. Three siblings can't agree on price or whether to list. A cash sale gives one number all parties can vote yes/no on.
  • Out-of-state executors who can't easily coordinate with agents, inspectors, and cleaners from another time zone.
  • Notre Dame-area properties with unique zoning, rental histories, or game-day-use complications.
