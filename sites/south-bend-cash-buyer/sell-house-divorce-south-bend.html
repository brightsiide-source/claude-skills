How Indiana Divorce Affects the House

Indiana is an equitable distribution state — the court divides marital assets fairly (not necessarily 50/50). The marital home is usually the largest single asset, and Indiana courts have wide discretion in how it gets allocated. There's typically a 60-day waiting period from petition filing before a divorce can be finalized.

How the house gets handled typically follows one of three paths:

  1. One spouse buys out the other and keeps the house. Usually requires a refinance to remove the other spouse from the mortgage, plus a cash settlement for their share.
  2. Both spouses agree to sell and split proceeds per the decree. Usually MLS-listed — 60-90 days.
  3. Cash sale to a third party for speed and certainty. Both parties sign at closing; proceeds split per decree; both walk away same day.

We're typically Path 3 — the cash buyer.

Why a Cash Sale Helps During Divorce

  • Speed. 14-30 day close means you're not paying a mortgage on a shared house through months of MLS.
  • No showings. Strangers walking through what's still your home during an already-difficult time.
  • Clean number. One firm cash offer both parties can accept or reject. No "buyer wants $5k off after inspection."
  • Same-day cash-out. Both parties get their share at closing per whatever split the decree specifies.
  • Confidentiality. No MLS listing online. No yard sign. No neighbors asking questions.
  • Reduced friction between spouses. No fights about staging, listing price, or accepting which offer.

Honest Note About Price

If both spouses are getting along, have time, and the house is in good shape — a traditional MLS listing will probably net more. Our offer is most compelling when speed, certainty, privacy, or reduced conflict matter more than the last few thousand dollars.
