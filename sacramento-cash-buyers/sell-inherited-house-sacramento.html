What to Know About California Probate

California probate is governed by the California Probate Code. In the Sacramento area, estates are administered through the Sacramento County Superior Court Probate Division. A few practical points:

  • California probate is slow. Full probate typically runs 9-18 months, sometimes longer for complex estates. It's one of the slowest probate processes in the country.
  • Simplified procedures for smaller estates. If the gross value of the estate (excluding certain assets) is under $184,500, a Small Estate Affidavit can avoid full probate. For real property under $61,500, an Affidavit of Real Property of Small Value works. Thresholds change periodically — check current limits.
  • Spousal Property Petition can transfer property to a surviving spouse without full probate.
  • The executor (called "personal representative" in CA) can sell during probate with court approval, usually after Letters Testamentary are issued and a Notice of Proposed Action is served.
  • You don't have to clean it out. We buy properties with the deceased's belongings still in them. Take family photos, jewelry, important documents. Leave the rest.
  • Out-of-state heirs are very common in Sacramento estates — many adult children live in the Bay Area, LA, or elsewhere. The entire transaction can be handled remotely.

Why Inherited Sacramento Houses Don't Sell Well on the MLS

  • Decades of deferred maintenance. Many Sacramento homes are 50-100+ years old — original kitchens, knob-and-tube wiring, aging roofs.
  • Stuff. Decades of life accumulated. MLS expects empty and clean.
  • Multi-heir disagreements. Three siblings can't agree on price, paint color, or whether to list. A cash sale gives one number all parties can vote yes/no on.
  • Out-of-state executors who can't easily coordinate with agents, inspectors, and cleaners from another time zone.
  • Properties tied up in extended probate where the timeline is unclear and MLS buyers won't wait.
