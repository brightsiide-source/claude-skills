How California Divorce Affects the House

California is a community property state. Assets and debts acquired during the marriage are generally owned equally by both spouses regardless of whose name is on the title or who paid the mortgage. There's a minimum 6-month waiting period from petition filing before a California divorce can be finalized.

The marital home is usually the largest single community asset, and how it's handled typically follows one of three paths:

  1. One spouse buys out the other and keeps the house. Usually requires a refinance to remove the other spouse from the mortgage, plus a cash settlement for their community-property share.
  2. Both spouses agree to sell and split proceeds per the Marital Settlement Agreement or court order. Usually MLS-listed — 60-90 days.
  3. Cash sale to a third party for speed and certainty. Both parties sign at closing; proceeds split per the agreement; both walk away same day.

We're typically Path 3 — the cash buyer. We've worked with sellers in every stage from "we just decided" to "the judgment is final and we need this done."

Why a Cash Sale Helps During a California Divorce

  • Speed. 14-30 day close means you're not paying a mortgage on a shared house for months of MLS.
  • No showings. Strangers walking through what's still your home during an already-difficult time.
  • Clean number. One firm cash offer both parties can accept or reject. No "buyer wants $5k off after inspection" re-negotiation.
  • Same-day cash-out. Both parties get their share at closing, wired by the title company per whatever community-property split the agreement or judgment specifies.
  • Confidentiality. No MLS listing photos online. No yard sign. No neighbors asking questions.
  • Reduced friction between spouses. No fights about staging, listing price, or accepting which offer.

Honest Note About Price

If both spouses are getting along, have time, and the house is in good shape — a traditional MLS listing will probably net more. Our offer is most compelling when speed, certainty, privacy, or reduced conflict matter more than the last few thousand dollars.
