What California Homeowners Should Know About Foreclosure

California is primarily a non-judicial foreclosure state. Most lenders use the Deed of Trust process, governed by California Civil Code §§ 2924-2924k. Compared to judicial-foreclosure states like Iowa or Indiana, the California timeline is fast:

  • Notice of Default — typically filed after 90 days of missed payments
  • Notice of Trustee's Sale — recorded 90+ days after the Notice of Default
  • Trustee's Sale — held at least 21 days after the Notice of Trustee's Sale is recorded
  • Total typical timeline: ~4-7 months from initial default to auction

That speed is why most traditional MLS listings can't close before the Trustee's Sale — a 60-90 day MLS process collides with the auction. Cash buyers close in 10-30 days.

Selling before the Trustee's Sale is materially better than letting the foreclosure complete:

  • A foreclosure stays on your credit report for 7 years. A regular sale doesn't.
  • You keep any equity above what's owed. At Trustee's Sales, properties typically sell for just enough to cover the loan and fees — any equity is gone.
  • Your future borrowing options are preserved.
  • You control the timing — closing date, move-out, what you take with you.

Honest Note About Equity

If you're significantly underwater on the mortgage — payoff greater than the house is worth — a short sale negotiated with your lender is usually better than a cash sale. We'll tell you that on the first call.

If you have any equity, even $10k-$30k, a cash sale before Trustee's Sale usually nets more than letting the foreclosure complete. At California Trustee's Sales, properties routinely sell for just the payoff amount.

Note: California also offers some seller protections under the Homeowner Bill of Rights — including pre-foreclosure outreach and loan modification options. A HUD-approved housing counselor (free) can walk you through them. If a modification is more appropriate than a sale, we'll point you toward that resource.
